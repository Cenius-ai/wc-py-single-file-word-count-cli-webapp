Counting words before you publish

Most editors ship a word count buried in a status bar. That is fine while you
are writing, and useless the moment you are scripting: a build step, a docs
lint, or a CI job that fails when a README drifts past its agreed budget.
wc.py exists for that narrow job. It takes one path, reads the text, splits it
on runs of whitespace -- spaces, tabs, newlines, and the non-breaking space
inside "&nbsp;" once decoded -- and prints a single integer.

The contract is intentionally boring. One argument in, one number out, zero
config, no network, and no writes. Errors are plain sentences on stderr with a
non-zero exit status, so a shell script can branch on the result without
parsing prose. Under the hood it is a hundred lines of standard-library Python,
which means it runs anywhere a Python 3 interpreter does.

A practical note on pacing: rerun the count after every structural edit rather
than at the end of the draft. Deltas are easier to reason about than totals,
and the habit keeps the final trim honest.

-- The wc-py maintainers
